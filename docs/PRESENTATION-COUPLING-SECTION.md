# Presentation narrative — Coupling section (as supplied 2026-08-26; source: round-3 realized artefacts)

## What we built, and what it taught us

**First, we built the coupled framework the problem statement specifies**: a 2D high-resolution terrain model coupled, cell-by-cell, to a graph hydraulic model of Bengaluru's stormwater drain network — street capture, subsurface routing, surcharge and return onto the street grid. Nothing in it is hand-waved: every capacity value, terminal sink and flow direction comes from a contract-checked loader reading BBMP-derived public map data — 1,721 junctions, 1,301 observed reaches and 286 synthesised connectors (routing paths derived from terrain drainage, not mapped drains), 1,208 capacity-bearing pipes routed.

**We ran it at full domain.** Twelve point seven million cells at ten-meter resolution, one hundred percent of the mappable network active, driven by a design storm, with mass conserved to a judged residual of seven parts in a hundred million. In the first twenty minutes of that storm, water lands on streets, is captured into 485 surcharging junctions of the network — reach nodes derived from geometry; this project holds no manhole dataset — and returns roughly 188,000 cubic meters to the surface. That 485 is the NODE count (38 nodes flagged by the pre-registered falsifier set plus 447 beside them); it coincides numerically with the surcharge EVENT count because each surcharging node produced exactly one event. The mechanism works, end to end, on the real city.

**Then we measured where the surcharge actually comes from — and found something decisive: 96.87% of surcharged volume originates on reaches that terminate. They have no outlet in BBMP's own map.** Just 3.13% sits on any path that could carry it out of the system.

**Before accepting that, we tested whether it was our own artifact.** Two things were checked separately. Extending the outfall DEFINITION itself — treating lakes as terminal sinks (Bellandur and Varthur are true endpoints) and counting boundary-crossing reaches as exiting — moved the dead-end share only from 97.28% to 96.87%, a 0.41-point shift that leaves the conclusion standing. And an independent sensitivity sweep of the mapping-boundary tolerance across five values, zero through fifty meters, came back completely flat: all five sweeps identical at 96.87%. The truncation lives in the public data, not in our classifier or our definition choices. And closing those gaps honestly is impossible: it would require inventing over ninety percent of the network's downstream connectivity, which we will not fabricate.

**Therefore we can demonstrate the mechanism, and we cannot yet claim Bengaluru's drains surcharge — the public map will not support the claim.** That is the correct finding from the best available ground truth, stated plainly. When a complete tertiary network exists, the same machinery runs on it unmodified.

**And here is the counterweight — why that day is servable the moment better data arrives.** Throughput is measured, not projected. On a one-sixteenth-area city tile — the natural operating unit for street-scale nowcasting — a complete twenty-minute simulated forecast window finishes in **twenty-three seconds of wall clock**, end to end, against a ten-minute operational gate. Scaling is super-linear: that tile costs two percent of the full-domain step cost while holding six percent of its cells (three-point-one times better than ideal). Quarter-domain tiles finish in under four minutes. Every rate cited was timed on the production code path, reproduced bit-for-bit between independent runs, with automatic gate refusals demonstrated working on day one. Compute was never the constraint. The city's own map was — and saying so precisely is the result.

One companion finding, same root cause, reported without dressing it up: the network is also too coarse to attribute flooding to specific streets. Across twenty-four verified flood points, the median distance to the nearest mapped drain segment is 272 meters, and exactly one point in twenty-four falls within the forty-meter radius pre-registered from drainage geometry *before* any match was counted. With tertiary connectivity excluded by the source data, street-level attribution from these maps is not currently defensible — another way the same missing ninety percent shows up, seen through a different instrument.
